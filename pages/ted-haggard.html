Ted Haggard

A restoration process with no actual power to enforce anything

New Life Church and the NAE presidency

Ted Haggard founded New Life Church in Colorado Springs in 1984, starting with around 20 people meeting in his home basement; it grew to roughly 14,000 attendees by 2006. In 1998 a 50,000-square-foot intercessory prayer complex, the World Prayer Center, opened on the church campus. From 2003 to November 2006 he served as president of the National Association of Evangelicals, representing roughly 30 million evangelicals across some 45,000 churches — one of the most influential positions in American evangelical Christianity at the time.

Some sources — mainly countercult/apologetics watchdog sites rather than mainstream press, so this is worth flagging as less firmly sourced than the rest of this article — describe Haggard attending meetings of C. Peter Wagner's International Coalition of Apostles at Wagner's own invitation, and quote him referring to himself and other pastors as "apostolic pastors" at a May 2000 conference hosted at New Life. If accurate, that would place him closer to this site's core subject matter than a typical evangelical leader; it's included here as a documented but not fully independently verified detail, not as settled fact.

The scandal

In early November 2006, days before a Colorado ballot measure on same-sex marriage, Denver-based escort Mike Jones publicly alleged a three-year sexual relationship with Haggard and said Haggard had purchased methamphetamine from him. Haggard initially denied any relationship. After a voicemail recording surfaced, he admitted to buying meth — "I bought it for myself but never used it... I was tempted, but I never used it" — while still denying sexual contact, saying he'd received only a massage. He resigned as NAE president within days. New Life Church's own investigating board went further, concluding "our investigation and Pastor Haggard's public statements have proven without a doubt that he has committed sexually immoral conduct," and removed him as pastor.

Restoration with no teeth

Haggard voluntarily entered a restoration process in January 2007 overseen by a small group of outside pastors, including Jack Hayford and Tommy Barnett. A year later, in February 2008, he simply ended it. New Life's response made the actual limits of that process explicit: the church said restoration was "incomplete" and it still believed he shouldn't return to vocational ministry — but it had no power to prevent him from doing exactly that. He and his wife began home prayer meetings in December 2009, and in June 2010 launched St. James Church as an independent congregation, with no denominational body or outside oversight structure standing over him at all.

In 2009, a separate account had already surfaced: former member Grant Haas alleged a 2006 hotel-room incident and inappropriate contact from Haggard; New Life's insurer arranged a confidential settlement reported at $179,000, which the church described as compassionate assistance rather than a payout, and which stopped once Haas spoke publicly in apparent violation of a confidentiality agreement. Haggard acknowledged an "inappropriate relationship" with Haas on CNN.

It happened again

In July 2022, Kirk "Seth" Sethman — a St. James elder Haggard himself had ordained in 2012 — publicly accused Haggard of asking a young church member he was counseling for addiction to buy him methamphetamine in 2012, and of inappropriate touching of at least two young men, one account describing conduct beginning in 2019 when the accuser was 16. Haggard denied the allegations, dismissed Sethman publicly, said "I'm going to keep doing exactly what I do," and refused elders' calls to step down — reportedly splitting the congregation. Around that time he began a new "microchurch," Storyhouse Church, operating out of his home. The pattern is the same one from 2008: an internal body raises concerns, Haggard disputes them, and there is no external authority that can actually remove him from ministry. This site could not confirm his status beyond mid-2022 reporting; if you know of more recent developments, that's exactly the kind of correction this site's Issues page exists for.
