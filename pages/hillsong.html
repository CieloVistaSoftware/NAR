Hillsong: Brian Houston & Carl Lentz

How the world's most influential charismatic worship brand came apart

Biographical and factual, not a musical critique — Hillsong's worship output (and its overlap with Bethel Church's own worship culture) shaped charismatic Christianity globally regardless of what happened to its leadership. This article covers what's documented about that leadership's conduct and the institution's decline. Where a claim is disputed or unresolved, that's stated rather than presented as settled.

What Hillsong built

Brian and Bobbie Houston founded Hillsong Church in a Sydney suburb in 1983. It grew into a global network — Hillsong NYC, LA, London, and others — with a worship-music arm whose songs became standard repertoire in charismatic and even mainstream evangelical churches worldwide. At its peak the network operated in roughly 30 countries with more than 150,000 congregants.

Brian Houston: concealment charge, acquittal, and a separate resignation

Brian's father, pastor Frank Houston, sexually abused a boy in the 1970s. Frank confessed to Brian around 1999 and died in 2004 without ever facing criminal charges himself; the abuse became public through Australia's 2014 Royal Commission into child sexual abuse. In August 2021, Brian Houston was charged under Section 316 of the New South Wales Crimes Act 1900 — concealing a serious indictable offence — for allegedly knowing of his father's abuse and not reporting it to police.

He was acquitted on August 17, 2023. The ruling did not turn on whether Houston knew about the abuse — the magistrate found it likely he did — but on whether he had a "reasonable excuse" not to report it: the court found it likely Houston believed the victim did not want the matter reported, which the statute recognizes as a legal excuse.

That case is legally and factually separate from why Houston actually left Hillsong. He stepped back from day-to-day leadership in January 2022 to focus on his legal defense, then resigned outright in March 2022, after a church board investigation into two unrelated personal-conduct complaints from roughly the preceding decade: flirtatious text messages to a staff member, which the board attributed to his dependence on prescription sleep medication, and a separate incident in which, disoriented after mixing an anti-anxiety prescription with alcohol, he entered the wrong hotel room and spent time with the woman staying there.

Carl Lentz: fired for an affair, then a documented pattern

Carl Lentz led Hillsong NYC and became a genuine celebrity pastor — tattooed, media-savvy, with Justin Bieber among his congregants. Houston fired him in November 2020, officially for "leadership issues," "breaches of trust," and a "recent revelation of moral failures" — specifically an extramarital affair with designer Ranin Karim, which became public after she discussed it publicly.

What followed wasn't a single lapse. In leaked internal audio, Houston himself said Lentz had "more than one affair" and called him "a narcissist." A leaked internal Hillsong NYC investigation report from April 2022 documented multiple extramarital affairs and "multiple incidents of consensual or non-consensual sexual interaction between church leaders and congregants, staff, volunteers." A 2017 internal complaint — three years before the firing — had already accused Lentz of making a volunteer "extremely uncomfortable" and alleged leaders were texting nude photos to female volunteers.

Leona Kimes, who had worked as a nanny for the Lentz family's own children for about seven years and later became co-pastor of Hillsong Boston with her husband Josh, publicly alleged in 2021 that Lentz subjected her to bullying, abuse of power, and both coerced-consensual and non-consensual sexual conduct between roughly 2015 and 2017. Lentz has acknowledged an "inappropriate relationship" with her but disputes the sexual-assault characterization. The Kimeses resigned as Hillsong Boston's pastors in April 2022 as the fallout continued.

Separately, whistleblower allegations — from Tasmanian MP Andrew Wilkie in March 2023 and from a former Hillsong governance coordinator's 2022 lawsuit — accused the organization of underreporting roughly $80 million in income and directing large cash gifts to the Houston family. Hillsong disputes this and points to a forensic audit it says found no unlawful conduct; no court or regulator has issued a public finding either way, so this remains a contested allegation, not an established fact.

What's left of Hillsong

The network has been contracting for years. Of 16 U.S. campuses, 11 have split off as independent churches, most recently Hillsong San Francisco in early 2025; departing pastors have publicly cited the Global Board's "heavy-handed control and secrecy," including pressure to sign NDAs and non-competes and a refusal to release the results of misconduct investigations. Hillsong London's lead pastors resigned without a stated reason after twelve years. In late 2025, Hillsong's Russian congregations rebranded as an independent church, "Legacy," citing both Russian legal restrictions on foreign-linked organizations and their own account of "turbulent and transitional" years — effectively ending Hillsong's presence there.

FX/Hulu's four-part documentary series The Secrets of Hillsong (May 2023), based on original Vanity Fair reporting, included the first interviews with Carl and Laura Lentz since his firing and examined what its producers describe as a long pattern of covering up misconduct to protect the institution.
