Robert Morris & Gateway Church

The most recent, and the one with an actual conviction

Unlike most cases on this site, this one has a criminal conviction and a guilty plea — not just allegations or a civil settlement. It's also still partly unresolved (a separate civil suit is ongoing as of this writing). Where sources disagree on a specific figure or date, that's stated rather than picked arbitrarily.

A major, politically connected megachurch

Robert Morris founded Gateway Church in Southlake, Texas, in 2000; it grew into a multi-campus megachurch with more than 39,000 active members and made him a bestselling author (The Blessed Life, 2002, among fourteen books). He chaired the board of The King's University, a Bible college tied to the church, and sat on Donald Trump's evangelical advisory board during the 2016 campaign, hosting a Trump campaign roundtable at Gateway's Dallas campus in 2020. He was not included on Trump's 2024 advisory board once the allegations below became public. Gateway's governance structure — organized around the "fivefold ministry" offices of apostle, prophet, evangelist, pastor, and teacher — has been characterized by critics as consistent with New Apostolic Reformation ecclesiology, though this site could not confirm Morris's personal ties to specific named apostolic networks with the same precision as some other figures covered here.

What Cindy Clemishire says happened

On June 14, 2024, the blog The Wartburg Watch — not, as sometimes reported, The Roys Report, though Roys Report went on to cover the story extensively — published Cindy Clemishire's account: that Morris, then a traveling evangelist in his early twenties and a friend of her family, began abusing her on Christmas night 1982, at her family's home in Osage County, Oklahoma. She was 12. By her own account, the abuse continued for roughly four and a half years.

Morris's public response, given to the Christian Post around June 17, 2024: "When I was in my early twenties, I was involved in inappropriate sexual behavior with a young lady in a home where I was staying. It was kissing and petting and not intercourse, but it was wrong. This behavior happened on several occasions over the next few years." That statement didn't disclose her age or acknowledge it as child abuse — it framed decades of alleged abuse of a 12-year-old as a moral failure with a "young lady." Clemishire's own response to Gateway's elder board accepting his resignation the next day: "Though I am grateful that he is no longer a pastor at Gateway, I am disappointed that the Board of Elders allowed him to resign."

What the internal investigation found

Gateway's elders hired the law firm Haynes & Boone to investigate; it reviewed 780 GB of data and thousands of documents, interviewing more than two dozen people — Morris himself declined to meet with them in person. The investigation found that all but three of Gateway's elders had some prior knowledge of the "encounter" and failed to inquire further, and that some elders reportedly knew before the story became public that Clemishire had been a child at the time. Gateway's own leadership called the outcome a "failure of culture." In November 2024, four elders were removed as a result; the board shrank from thirteen members to three.

Indictment, guilty plea, sentence

In March 2025, an Oklahoma grand jury indicted Morris on five felony counts of lewd or indecent acts with a child — Oklahoma's statute of limitations didn't bar the case because Morris had never been an Oklahoma resident. Attorney General Gentner Drummond: "There can be no tolerance for those who sexually prey on children. This case is all the more despicable because the alleged perpetrator was a pastor who exploited his position of trust and authority." Clemishire: "After almost 43 years, the law has finally caught up with Robert Morris for the horrific crimes he committed against me as a child."

On October 2, 2025, Morris pleaded guilty to all five counts. He was sentenced to 10 years, suspended after 6 months served in the Osage County Jail, followed by roughly 9.5 years of probation to be served in Texas, and was ordered to register as a sex offender. Restitution to Clemishire was ordered — reported figures range from $250,000 to $270,000 depending on the outlet, and this site isn't going to pick one without confirming it against the actual court order. His attorney said Morris "pled guilty because he wanted to accept responsibility for his conduct." Clemishire: "Today justice has finally been served, and the man who manipulated, groomed and abused me as a 12-year-old innocent girl is finally going to be behind bars. My hope is that many victims hear my story, and it can help lift their shame and allow them to speak up." He was released from jail in April 2026 to begin probation, stating: "What I did to Cindy decades ago was wrong. There is no other word for it, and there is no excuse for it. I am deeply sorry."

What's still unresolved

Clemishire and her father separately filed a civil defamation suit against Morris, his wife, and Gateway's leadership, alleging they knowingly mischaracterized the abuse as a consensual relationship with a "young lady" to protect the church's image. A judge has denied motions to dismiss it; as of this writing it's proceeding toward trial. The Oklahoma legislature's House unanimously passed the "Cindy Clemishire Act," which would void NDAs in child sexual abuse cases and eliminate the statute of limitations for crimes against children — it had advanced to the state Senate as of the most recent reporting available to this site, with its final status not yet confirmed. Gateway Church went roughly a year without a permanent senior pastor before naming Daniel and Tammie Floyd to the role in August 2025.
