Introduction

Why this site exists, and what to expect from it

I spent roughly 25 years inside the charismatic movement — not visiting it, not studying it from a seminary library, but inside it: in the services, in the prayer meetings, in the conversations that happen after the service ends and the real theology comes out. That's the vantage point this site is written from.

Most writing about the New Apostolic Reformation comes from one of two places. There's the insider material — books, sermons, conference recordings — written to promote and defend it. And there's the outsider material — academic papers, investigative journalism — written by people who never sat in the room. Both have real value. Neither is what this site is trying to do.

What this site is

This is a critique, written by someone who was close enough to see how the theology actually functions in practice, not just how it reads in a book. I think the central claims of the New Apostolic Reformation — that the offices of apostle and prophet continue today with real governing authority, and that Christians are mandated to seek control over society's institutions — are wrong. The first article lays out why in detail, biblically and practically.

I'm not trying to hide that position behind a false neutrality. But I do owe readers — including readers who disagree with me — an accurate account of what NAR actually teaches before I argue against it, and a clear line between what I witnessed firsthand and what I'm citing from someone else's research. Both of those are promises I intend to keep across every article here, not just this one.

What to expect

More articles will follow, going deeper into specific figures, specific claims, and specific consequences — always searchable, always sourced where the claim isn't personal experience. Start with the overview of the NAR itself, or browse and search everything published so far.
