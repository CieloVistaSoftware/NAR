# Timeline

A rough chronology connecting the Shepherding movement to the New Apostolic
Reformation. Dates for organizational milestones are approximate where sources
don't agree precisely; see [Sources](/public/doc-viewer.html?file=docs%2Fsources.md) and [People](/public/doc-viewer.html?file=docs%2Fpeople.md) for
the underlying references.

- **1970s** — The Shepherding (Discipleship) movement takes shape around Derek
  Prince, Bob Mumford, Don Basham, Charles Simpson, and Ern Baxter (the "Fort
  Lauderdale" teachers), teaching direct personal submission to an assigned
  "shepherd."
- **Early 1980s** — Public controversy over the movement's practices grows; Derek
  Prince distances himself from the group over concerns about how the teaching was
  being applied, while the other teachers continue.
- **1989** — The remaining Fort Lauderdale leaders issue a public statement
  acknowledging errors in how shepherding authority had been taught and practiced.
- **1990s–2000s** — C. Peter Wagner, drawing on trends he observed in global
  Pentecostal/charismatic Christianity, begins using and promoting the term "New
  Apostolic Reformation" to describe networks led by figures he identifies as
  functioning apostles and prophets.
- **2000s–2010s** — Apostolic networks and ministries associated with the term grow:
  Generals International, Harvest International Ministry, Bethel Church's media and
  training arms, TheCall's mass prayer gatherings, and others.
- **2016** — C. Peter Wagner dies; leadership of his institutional legacy passes to
  Ché Ahn.
- **January 6, 2021** — Journalists and researchers, most thoroughly Matthew D.
  Taylor, document the presence of NAR-associated imagery, leaders, and prophetic
  rhetoric at the U.S. Capitol events, drawing wider public and political attention
  to the movement's Seven Mountain Mandate teaching.
- **Ongoing** — This site is part of that ongoing public conversation, written from
  firsthand experience with the wider charismatic movement. See the
  [Introduction](?page=introduction) for that background, and the
  [main article](?page=nar) for the current state of the theological argument.
