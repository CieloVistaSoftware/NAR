# Glossary

Terms used throughout this site, defined the way they're actually used here — not
necessarily the way any single source defines them, since usage varies.

## New Apostolic Reformation (NAR)

A term, generally attributed to C. Peter Wagner, for a loose network of charismatic
churches and leaders who teach that the New Testament offices of apostle and prophet
continue today with real governing authority. Not a denomination — no membership roll,
no shared headquarters. See the [full article](?page=nar).

## Continuationism / Cessationism

**Continuationism** is the belief that all New Testament spiritual gifts (prophecy,
healing, tongues, and the offices of apostle and prophet) continue unchanged today.
**Cessationism** is the belief that some or all of those gifts and offices ended with
the apostolic era — usually with the death of the last apostle who had personally
witnessed the resurrected Christ, or with the completion of the New Testament canon.
Most of the theological critique of NAR on this site comes from a cessationist
argument specifically about the offices of apostle and prophet, not a blanket
rejection of every charismatic gift.

## Apostle (NAR usage)

In NAR usage, a recognized leader who holds governing authority over a network of
churches and ministries, analogous to (and sometimes explicitly modeled on) the New
Testament apostles. This site's central objection is that the New Testament ties the
apostolic office to a specific, closed qualification — personally witnessing the
resurrected Christ (Acts 1:21–22, 1 Corinthians 9:1, 1 Corinthians 15:8) — that this
usage doesn't meet.

## Prophet / Prophecy (NAR usage)

A recognized leader (or, more broadly, any believer) who delivers messages claimed to
be direct revelation from God, often including specific, dated predictions about
individuals, nations, or events.

## Seven Mountain Mandate

The teaching that Christians are called to seek influence over seven spheres of
society: religion, family, education, government, media, arts/entertainment, and
business. The aspect of NAR theology most closely tied to Christian dominionism and
to the political entanglement discussed in the [main article](?page=nar).

## Dominionism

The belief that Christians should actively seek to hold and exercise governing
authority over civil society, rather than merely have influence within it. Critics
apply this label to the Seven Mountain Mandate; NAR proponents typically reject the
label while defending the underlying teaching.

## Strategic-Level Spiritual Warfare (SLSW)

A set of practices — prayer against "territorial spirits," "prophetic mapping" of
geographic areas, public acts of "identificational repentance" — taught by C. Peter
Wagner and others as a companion practice to NAR's evangelistic goals.

## Shepherding (Discipleship) Movement

An earlier, related movement from the 1970s–80s, associated with Derek Prince, Bob
Mumford, Don Basham, Charles Simpson, and Ern Baxter (the "Fort Lauderdale" or "Fort
Lauderdale Five" teachers), which taught that every believer should be in a direct
relationship of submission to a personal "shepherd." See the [Introduction](?page=introduction)
for how this connects to this site's own background, and [People](/public/doc-viewer.html?file=docs%2Fpeople.md) for
more on the individual teachers.

## Apostolic Network

A group of churches and ministries connected not by shared denominational governance
but by relational submission to a recognized apostle or apostolic leader. Examples
discussed on this site include Generals International, Harvest International
Ministry, and the International Coalition of Apostolic Leaders (ICAL).
